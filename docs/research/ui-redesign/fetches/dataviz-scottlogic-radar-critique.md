# A critique of radar charts (Graham Odds, Scott Logic, 2011)

> Fetched 2026-10-19 · Practitioner critique of radar/spider charts. Read because a 5-axis "strengths profile" radar is the obvious visual for 5 categories on the Results screen and share card; this is the strongest case against it.

## What it says

Area exaggeration:

> "the area of the shapes increases as a square of the values rather than linearly"

Axis ordering is arbitrary and changes the shape:

> "we could arbitrarily change the relationships by rearranging the categories"

Cross-axis comparison is effortful:

> "comparison in radar charts requires conscious thought to mentally project a sort of arc of rotation"

Multiple series occlude each other:

> "the design of radar charts means significant occlusion is inevitable when multiple series are plotted"

The no-fill variant "suffers least from occlusion". The conclusion calls radar charts "ultimately useless". The only exception it allows to the nominal-scale critique is "when plotting timeseries data".

## What it does not say

- No controlled study of reader accuracy is cited; this is expert opinion.
- It does not consider the use case here: one series, 5 axes on the same 0–100% scale, used as an emblem of a "profile" rather than for reading values. The search snippets from other practitioner pages (Exceljet: "at-a-glance" strengths and weaknesses; QuestionPro: keep variables few) say radar is defensible for one entity with few same-scale axes, but those pages were not fetched in full and are not cited as evidence.
