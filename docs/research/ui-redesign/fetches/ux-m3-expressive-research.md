# Better, Easier, Emotional UX — the research behind Material 3 Expressive

> Fetched 2026-10-19 · Google Design's write-up of the user research (46 studies, 18,000+ participants) behind M3 Expressive; read to know what is actually evidenced vs. claimed, and the stated limits.

## What it says

Scale and method:

> "Through 46 separate research studies with hundreds of designs, and more than 18,000 participants from around the world, we've fine tuned a system that's both beautiful and highly usable."

> "The fundamental parts of expressive design are the use of color, shape, size, motion, and containment."

> "We evaluated options for progress indicators, assessing which made waiting time feel faster while also looking like it belonged on a premium phone. We studied how big a button can be, seeking improvements in tap time without overwhelming other items on the screen."

> "In many cases, we chose to exceed existing standards for tap target size, color contrast, and other important aspects that can make interfaces easier to use."

Preference (age skew relevant to a young Brazilian audience):

> "We found that well-applied, expressive design is strongly preferred by people of all ages, with that preference being particularly strong — up to 87% — among 18-to-24-year-olds."

> "M3 Expressive designs were overwhelmingly rated higher for attributes such as "energetic," "emotive," "positive vibe," "creative," "playful," and "friendly.""

Usability (eye tracking):

> "We brought a diverse set of people into a lab with the very latest eye-tracking glasses and had them interact with 10 different apps in both Material 3 Expressive and current Material 3 versions, randomly ordered. Participants were able to spot key UI elements up to four times faster in the M3 Expressive designs, suggesting that they steer user attention toward the most important part of the screen."

> "For example, the Send button in the new design (on the right) is larger, placed just above the keyboard, and uses a secondary color to draw attention to it. [...] When participants were asked to "send the email," their eyes saw the button four times faster in the expressive design."

> "But with M3 Expressive versions, we've seen a dramatic erasure of age effects in fixation times, helping 45-plus-year-old users perform on par with their younger counterparts."

Limits stated by Google (counter-evidence):

> "One design we tested didn't [sic: page reads "didn’'t"] stick to the patterns and standards of our design system: For a playlist, we replaced the familiar vertically scrolling list of songs with images from album art arranged helter-skelter. While users reported the interface looked modern and exciting, usability scores suffered. In another example, removing text labels from email actions resulted in decreased usability."

> "When basic interaction paradigms are broken, expressive design can lead to poor usability or negative sentiment."

> "It is also worth noting that while expressive design improved preference and usability, it was impacted by users' lack of familiarity."

> "Be intentional and start from user need: While there was broad appeal for vibrant, expressive design, a strong minority of users preferred calmer, less intense versions."

> "Prioritize functionality: Don't compromise your product's core functionality for visual flourishes. No amount of emotion can compensate for a lack of clarity."

## What it does not say

- No per-study sample sizes, effect sizes, or which progress indicator variant "made waiting time feel faster" (the wavy one is implied by the launch, but not stated here).
- No data on retention, session length, or completion; the evidence is preference, desirability attributes and fixation/tap time.
- Nothing on Brazil specifically ("participants from around the world" only).
