# Mobile-App Onboarding: An Analysis of Components and Techniques (NN/g, Alita Kendrick, 2020-06-21)

> Fetched 2026-10-19 · Nielsen Norman Group's analysis of onboarding components; read to decide whether the Home screen should have any intro/tutorial before "Start test".

## What it says

> "Generally speaking, onboarding is problematic for a few reasons: Higher interaction cost. [...] Memory strain. [...] May not improve user performance. [...] our research on deck-of-cards tutorials, a method of instructional onboarding, showed that tutorials didn't improve task performance."

> "Consequently, we recommend professionals avoid creating app onboarding whenever possible and instead spend your resources making the UI more usable."

> "For most mobile apps, users should be able to learn the interface by using it and thus instructional onboarding flows are not necessary."

> "Avoid feature-promotion onboarding at first launch. Users rarely download an app for no reason; therefore, lengthy promotional onboarding will likely be skipped."

> "Instead of front-loading users with this information, present these types of promotional screens on the app store page, as that is where users will explore new apps and compare features."

> "In particular, visual-design customization, such as selecting a color scheme, doesn't belong in onboarding. It's hard for people to know how they will prefer the app to look before actually using it [...] (Also, in general, a lot of research shows that people usually stick with defaults.)"

> "Deck-of-cards tutorials [...] particularly in relatively simple mobile apps, tends to make the interface appear more complicated than it actually is, and strains user's memory. Therefore, we don't recommend deck-of-cards onboarding."

> "When done successfully, (i.e., brief and only highlighting what's new or unfamiliar, as mentioned above) interactive walkthroughs feel more like a practice round and less like a tutorial."

> "Keep onboarding as simple as possible. For most mobile apps, this means putting users directly into the interface."

## What it does not say

- No quantitative data on start rates or first-session drop-off with vs. without onboarding.
- Nothing about entertainment/quiz apps or about the content of a Home screen's value proposition (headline, "30 questions, ~X min" framing). The "start quickly" recommendation is the inference; the article does not discuss expectation-setting copy (e.g. stating test length up front).
