# Bilker et al. (2012), "Development of Abbreviated Nine-item Forms of the Raven's Standard Progressive Matrices Test", Assessment 19(3):354–369

> Fetched 2026-10-19 · Peer-reviewed paper (PMC full text). Read for what it reports about timed vs untimed Raven administration, and for whether a short form preserves the full-test score. Read through WebFetch's summariser; quotes are as returned.

## What it says

- "The RSPM, as well as the APM, can be administered as a speeded test."
- Speeded version defined as "setting a fixed time limit for the test administration (the speeded test)."
- Citing Hamel and Schmittmann (2006): "r = .75 between 20 minute limit scores and non-timed scores" on the APM.
- Citing Hamel and Schmittmann: "for the untimed test, practice and experience with previous items play a large role in performance."
- Citing Raven, Raven and Court (1993): "as a speeded test, intellectual efficiency is assessed."
- "Raven, Raven and Court (1998) found that the untimed version of the APM has a one dimensional factor structure", whereas a timed version "invariably includes a dimension for speed."
- APM administration times listed in its Table 9: 45 minutes (manual-based samples) and 34 minutes (one validation sample).
- Abstract: "a 9-item subset of RSPM predicts the total score for the 60-item scale with good accuracy."
- Limits: "There does appear to be a ceiling effect, particularly for Form B." and "Issues pertaining to validity are the Achilles heel to all short-forms, ours notwithstanding."

## What it does not say

- Anything about scoring response time per item: the timed and untimed forms are both scored as number correct; the time limit only caps how many items are attempted.
