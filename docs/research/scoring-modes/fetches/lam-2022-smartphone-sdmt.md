# Lam et al. (2022), "Reliability, construct and concurrent validity of a smartphone-based cognition test in multiple sclerosis", Multiple Sclerosis Journal 28(2)

> Fetched 2026-10-19 · Peer-reviewed validation of a self-administered smartphone Symbol Digit Modalities Test (a Coding-like processing-speed task) run on participants' own phones. Read as evidence that a tap-based 90 s speed task works on consumer phones, and for its practice effect. Read through WebFetch's summariser; quotes are as returned.

## What it says

- The sSDMT was "performed by tapping the digits corresponding to each shown symbol on the smartphone screen."
- Score: "The number of correct responses after a 90-second trial."
- "The symbol–digit combination is randomised in each trial."
- The app "was installed on the own smartphones of the participants during the baseline visit."
- "Reliability estimates of the sSDMT were high (ICCs > 0.80)."
- "a score change of 6.7 points or more can be distinguished as a change beyond measurement error."
- Correlation with the clinic SDMT: described as "strong" in the text and "modest" in the abstract (highest r = 0.690).
- "The weekly averaged sSDMT scores gradually increased during the follow-up period" — practice effects, not modelled.
- A software bug slowed the test and excluded 11 patients: "this should prompt continuous alertness for occurrences of technical issues in future uses of technology-based biomarkers." Home testing "may also be accompanied with more distractions during testing compared to in-clinic testing." Screen size was not a significant confounder.

## What it does not say

- Norms for the general population (MS patients and controls only).
- How interruptions mid-trial were handled.
