# Carlozzi, Beaumont, Tulsky & Gershon (2015), "The NIH Toolbox Pattern Comparison Processing Speed Test: Normative Data", Archives of Clinical Neuropsychology 30(5):359–368

> Fetched 2026-10-19 · Peer-reviewed normative paper (PMC full text) for a short, screen-based processing-speed task. Read as the closest published model for a tap-based speed task in a phone app. Read through WebFetch's summariser; quotes are as returned.

## What it says

- Task: participants "identify whether two visual patterns are the "same" or "not the same"", responding by "pressing a "yes" or "no" button".
- "Scores reflected the number of correct items (of a possible 130) completed in 90 s"
- "The NIHTB Pattern Comparison Processing Test is a 90-s test designed to evaluate PS across the lifespan (ages 3–85)."
- "Patterns were either identical or varied on one of three dimensions" (colour, adding/taking something away, one versus many — the last for ages 3–15 only); items "were designed to minimize the number of errors that were made", and items below 75% accuracy in development were dropped.
- "Overall, ICC for test–retest reliability for the NIHTB Pattern Comparison Processing Speed Test was 0.61" (1-week retest, N = 513, mean gain 5.5 points: a practice effect).
- Discussion: "a brief, sensitive measure of PS that is both valid and reliable."

## What it does not say

- Correlations with WAIS Coding/Symbol Search (its convergent measures are other NIH Toolbox tasks).
- Anything about pausing: the 90 s is one uninterrupted block.
- A search result (ClinicalTrials.gov record, not fetched) gave 85 s of response time for the iPad version; unverified here.
